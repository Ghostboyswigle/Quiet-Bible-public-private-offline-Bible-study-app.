Quiet Bible - Other research documents (Library > Research)
==========================================================

The documents in assets/www/bible/docs/ were chosen because the Ben Born Again channel
(https://www.youtube.com/@1BENBORNAGAIN, https://benbornagain.faith) talks about them. The
descriptions are Quiet Bible's; "What the channel says" sums up the channel's own view as
published on its website. Quiet Bible is not affiliated with the channel.

study/tools/build_docs.py in Quiet Bible's source code builds them from the sources below.
It splits the texts into parts and sections and removes navigation, page numbers, images and
markup other than italics, bold and note numbers. It does not change the wording.

The Septuagint in English (Brenton)
  Sir Lancelot C. L. Brenton's translation (1844), public domain, in the LXX2012 edition
  ("Septuagint in American English 2012", Michael Paul Johnson, eBible.org), public domain.
  Files from https://github.com/BibleNLP/ebible (tests/versifications/eng-lxx2012).

The Masoretic text (Hebrew)
  Westminster Leningrad Codex, public domain, as published by the Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible
  project, https://github.com/openscriptures/morphhb (only the Hebrew text is used; its lemma
  and morphology data, CC BY 4.0, are not).

The Geneva Bible (1599), the Textus Receptus (1550/1894), the Wycliffe Bible (about 1395)
  Public domain texts, from CrossWire's SWORD modules as converted by scrollmapper
  bible_databases, https://github.com/scrollmapper/bible_databases (MIT licence for the
  conversion).

The Apocrypha of the 1611 King James Bible
  King James Version with Apocrypha (1769 text), public domain, from scrollmapper
  bible_databases as collected by Open Christian Data,
  https://github.com/eve-coda/open-christian-data (data marked public domain).

1 Clement, 2 Clement, the Didache, Barnabas, Ignatius, Polycarp, Diognetus, Hermas,
Justin Martyr
  Translations from the Ante-Nicene Fathers (Roberts, Donaldson and others, 1885-1896),
  public domain, from the Historical Christian Faith Writings Database,
  https://github.com/HistoricalChristianFaith/Writings-Database ("All works in this repo are
  in the public domain").

Josephus (William Whiston, 1737), Tacitus, Annals 15.44 (A. J. Church and W. J. Brodribb,
1876), the Babylonian Talmud (Michael L. Rodkinson, 1896-1903), the Zohar (Nurho de Manhar,
1900-1914), the Sefer Yetzirah (W. Wynn Westcott, 1887), the Lesser Key of Solomon
(S. L. MacGregor Mathers and Aleister Crowley, 1904)
  Public domain texts (in the United States at least), as scanned, proofed and published by
  the Internet Sacred Text Archive, https://sacred-texts.com (John Bruno Hare), taken from the
  archive's March 2023 mirror at https://github.com/Neckr0ik/Sacred-Texts.com (MIT licence for
  the mirror). The archive's notices of attribution are kept on the title pages inside each
  document.
  The notices on Books 1 and 4 of the Talmud and on the Zohar read: "This file may be used for
  any non-commercial purpose, provided this attribution is left intact" (or "redistributed for
  non-commercial purposes"). These three are therefore NOT covered by Quiet Bible's GPL
  licence: they may be used and shared for non-commercial purposes only, with the notice left
  intact (it is kept on their title pages). Quiet Bible is free. Anyone who wants to sell a copy
  of Quiet Bible must first remove them from web/bible/docs/ in the source (talmud/0.json and
  3.json, the zohar folder, and their entries in index.json). The other documents
  from the archive carry no such notice.

Not included
  The Dead Sea Scrolls, Aryeh Kaplan's Sefer Yetzirah, the Soncino Zohar and Talmud, Shem
  Tov's Hebrew Matthew, Tyndale's 1525 New Testament, Pliny's letter on the Christians in
  Betty Radice's translation, and modern books and articles the channel quotes, because they
  are under copyright or no free copy could be found.
